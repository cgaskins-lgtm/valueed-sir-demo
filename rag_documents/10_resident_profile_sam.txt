RESIDENT PROFILE — TRAINING SCENARIO (FICTIONAL)
All names, dates, and identifying details are fictional and for training purposes only.

NAME: Sam (first name only used per confidentiality protocol)
AGE: 17
GRADE: 9th
GENDER: Male
PLACEMENT TYPE: Residential Treatment

PLACEMENT HISTORY:
Sam has been in residential placement for approximately 8 months. Prior placements were disrupted due to physical altercations with peers and staff. He was placed at this facility following a court order related to assault charges.

FAMILY/GUARDIAN INFORMATION:
- Legal Guardian: Mother (referred to as "Sam's mother" or "the guardian" in all documentation)
- Sam has a strong emotional attachment to his mother. Contact with her is a significant motivator and a known trigger when contact is denied or interrupted.
- Mother's visitation and phone contact are governed by a structured schedule. Unscheduled contact requests are common and require careful management.

KNOWN TRIGGERS:
1. Denial of or delays in contact with his mother — the most significant trigger
2. Feeling disrespected, dismissed, or "talked down to" by staff
3. Peer conflict, especially with residents he has previously fought with
4. Perceived unfairness or inconsistency in staff decisions
5. Being told "no" without explanation or without being offered an alternative
6. Feeling cornered or physically blocked

DE-ESCALATION STRATEGIES THAT WORK FOR SAM:
- Offer choices rather than directives ("Would you like to walk outside or sit in the office?")
- Validate his feelings before redirecting ("I understand you're upset about the visit. That's hard.")
- Give him physical space — do not follow him closely when he asks to be alone
- Avoid issuing consequences during escalation — address behavior after he is calm
- Peer support from a trusted staff member he has rapport with
- Allow him to write or draw to express himself when verbal communication breaks down

DE-ESCALATION STRATEGIES THAT DO NOT WORK:
- Issuing threats or consequences while he is escalating
- Physically blocking his path
- Following him after he asks to be left alone
- Arguing about rules or fairness during escalation
- Involving peers who have conflict history with Sam in the same space during his escalation

BEHAVIORAL HISTORY:
- Two prior physical altercations with peers in the last 30 days
- Three prior restraints in the past 90 days, all related to perceived denial of rights or family contact
- No history of self-harm
- No current mental health crisis designation

STRENGTHS:
- Strong verbal skills when calm
- Responds well to staff who are consistent and respectful
- Expresses remorse after incidents when given space to process
- Motivated by family contact and privileges

CLINICAL NOTES FOR STAFF:
Sam frequently tests staff consistency. Power struggles are a high-risk dynamic with this resident. Staff should be coached to avoid "winning" confrontations with Sam and instead focus on de-escalation and relationship preservation. When Sam feels respected, his compliance and engagement improve significantly.
